# Posterior / Anterior / Upper — Strength Split

**Framework:** kinetic chain. Posterior = back, triceps, glutes/hams, rear delt, calves. Anterior = chest, delts (side/front), biceps, quads, tib, abs. Upper = second bench + back/arm exposure.

**Set rule:** 2 sets per exercise, *except barbell bench* — 3 sets on the anterior (primary) day, because it's the lift driving the 100 kg goal and 2 is thin for strength.

**Why it's built lopsided:** swimming (2–3×/week) already dumps heavy volume into back, lats, and rear delts. Nothing does that for your press. So the gym deliberately protects pressing and arms, and keeps standalone back work leaner than it looks — the pool makes up the difference.

## Weekly volume (gym only — before run/swim stack on top)

| Area | Sets/wk | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | 8–9 | bench 2×/wk + pec deck |
| Delts | ~6 | side/front on anterior+upper, rear on posterior |
| Back | ~8 | posterior 4 + upper 4 — **your heaviest gym area, plus swimming** |
| Biceps | 6 | antagonist-fresh (trained away from rows) |
| Triceps | 6 | antagonist-fresh (trained away from presses) |
| Quads | 4 | + running/swimming load |
| Hams/glutes | 4 | + single-leg / left-hip work |
| Calves | 2–4 | |

**One flag on the added back volume:** ~8 gym sets *plus* 2–3 swims puts back/lats/rear-delt as by far your most-trained region. That's fine if hypertrophy there is a want — just watch pull-day and hard-swim fatigue landing together, and if your shoulders feel stacked, the Upper-day pulldown is the first thing to trim.

---

## Notes specific to this block

- **Barbell bench must actually be barbell.** Machine incline and dips don't drive a barbell-bench 1RM. Bench is the anchor on both press exposures.
- **On a cut with heavy cardio, 2 sets is the right call** for retention — load matters more than set count here. Bench is the only lift worth 3.
- **Don't stack heavy leg work against your long run or hardest swim.** Leg-day soreness quietly taxes both, and run + swim are co-priorities this block.

---

## If you had 4 days — the optimum is Posterior / Anterior / Posterior / Anterior

Cleaner than P/A/U in your own framework, and it fixes the one weakness (quads/hams only hit 1× on the 3-day):

- **Everything reaches 2× frequency** — the biggest single driver of strength and retention — including the legs P/A/U shortchanges.
- **No "Upper" patch day needed** — its whole job was buying back a second bench/upper exposure that one P + one A can't give. Four days give that for free.
- Make the two Anterior days **heavy/low-rep bench** vs **rep-focused bench**; same split for the pull across the two Posterior days.

**Honest trade-off:** P/A/P/A is the better *program* but the worse *fit for this block*. Four lifting days on top of swimming ramping to 3× and running, in a deficit, is real recovery load. Run the 4-day at maintenance or off-season — not mid-cut with pool volume climbing. If you try it now, watch sleep and resting HR and be willing to drop the 4th day if either drifts.
