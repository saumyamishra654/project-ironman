Why this block

Keeps the existing 3-run week. The goal is a faster, more economical 5K — which is not a detour from the Goa 70.3, it's foundational to it: your run-leg limiter is running economy + velocity at threshold + cadence under fatigue, and those are exactly what a 5K block builds. A faster 5K lifts every longer pace underneath it.

Structure adapted from the plan Saumya flagged: Run 1 = sub-target intervals, Run 2 = a growing tempo ladder at 10K pace, Run 3 = long run with race-pace intervals — mapped onto the current speed / quality / long-run week.

The two limiters

Hips / cadence biomechanical

Years of quad-dominant running mean that under fatigue you reach with the quads and turnover collapses instead of driving from the hips/glutes. Seen in every run — cadence decays as you tire, sits stuck ~153.
Fix: turnover training (strides, cadence cues) + posterior-chain strength — already fed by the Posterior lift day. Bonus: higher cadence / shorter stride is also shin insurance.

Holding pace cardiovascular

Can hit 6:00/km but not hold it past ~2 km. That pace currently sits above threshold, so it turns anaerobic fast and lactate accumulates faster than it clears — hence the 2 km cliff.
Fix: drag threshold up toward 6:00 (tempo at 10K pace) and lift the VO₂ ceiling above it (the 5:30 intervals). The tempo ladder is the single biggest lever here.

Run 1 — Speed / VO₂

Faster than goal pace (~5:30–5:45/km) to raise the ceiling and teach turnover. Full-ish recoveries — quality, not threshold. If form breaks on a rep, the session is done.

Run 3 — Long run + race-pace

Stays aerobic and HR-capped (base discipline holds), but sprinkle goal 5K pace into the back half so 6:00 starts to feel normal, not frantic. Well within the 21 km cap.

  • Early weeks: last 3 km as 3 × 1 km @ 6:00, easy 400 m between.
  • Later weeks: 5–6 × (1 km @ race pace / 1 km easy).

Add-ons that punch above their weight

Strides 2× / week

6–8 × 20s at ~mile effort, full recovery, after an easy portion. The cheapest economy + cadence gains there are, and the most direct antidote to the hip/turnover problem. Shin-friendly.

Cadence cues each easy run

One metronome / "quick feet" segment at 165–170 spm in short bursts, until faster turnover is default rather than something you have to think about. Don't force pace — just turn the legs over a hair quicker.
