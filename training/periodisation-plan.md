### Endurance-first hybrid identity

**Plan:** Primary goals are endurance (95th-percentile running and triathlon); lifting is held at the 80th percentile ('Advanced').

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [1], [4], [49], [50], [52], [60]

Lifting and endurance can coexist: across 43 studies adding aerobic work did not reduce maximal strength or hypertrophy gains [1], and an umbrella review of 144 studies found concurrent and single-mode training comparable for strength, hypertrophy and aerobic capacity [60]. The exception is lower-body strength in trained men (SMD -0.43) [4] and explosive strength (SMD -0.28) [1], which is why lifting is capped at Advanced rather than Elite. The endurance targets themselves come from race-result distributions [49][50][52].

### One primary target per phase; everything else on a minimum dose

**Plan:** Each phase develops one quality hard and maintains the others with the minimum effective dose (1-2 heavy lifting sessions/week).

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [8], [9], [24], [55], [57]

One session a week with one hard set per exercise maintained 1RM for 8-32 weeks in 20-35-year-olds [8], and once-weekly heavy lifting preserved strength and thigh size in well-trained cyclists through a 13-week season [9]. Block periodisation of endurance shows small advantages (VO2max ES 0.40) but from short, low-quality studies [24]. No study tests decade-long goal sequencing, so the phase order itself rests on the peak-age data below; the principle of developing one quality while maintaining the others is also standard practitioner advice [55][57].

### Strength year at 23-24, before the speed window

**Plan:** Dec 2027 - Nov 2028 is the main lifting block, not 27-31 as first drafted.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [7], [13], [18]

Classic powerlifters peak at about age 27 as a cohort and after 4-6 years of competing [13], so strength started at 23 is still rising at the speed-window age. Heavy strength also transfers to speed: non-specific strength and plyometric training improve sprint times (~2.6%) [18] and strength training improves maximal sprint speed in runners [7]. Lifting heavy first therefore helps the next phase instead of competing with it.

### Speed window at 24-27

**Plan:** Mile, 5K, 400 m and 100 m are the primary goals from Dec 2028 to Nov 2031.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [10], [11], [15]

World-class sprinters and middle-distance runners peak at about 24.5-25 [10][11]; speed and power peak earliest and decline first [11]. These are lifelong athletes, so the exact age carries uncertainty for an adult starter, but explosive qualities follow calendar age more than training age, unlike endurance [15].

### Road and 70.3 time goals at 27-29

**Plan:** HM, marathon and fast 70.3 goals come after the speed window.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [10], [15], [44]

Marathon peak age is ~28-29 [10]; in 70.3 data, men under 30 are fastest [44]. Endurance development is driven by training years (elite athletes need 10-15 years) [15], so an athlete who started at 22 benefits from waiting. Running economy also keeps improving with years of running.

### Norseman last, with ballot entries every year

**Plan:** First full Ironman ~2033, Norseman 2033-2037; enter the Norseman ballot every October from 2026.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [12], [47]

Ironman performance peaks at 32-35 in men [12]. Norseman places are a supply problem: 125 of 335 places go by ballot, tickets accumulate 1-2-3 over consecutive unsuccessful years and reset if a year is skipped or you are drawn [47]. Entering yearly from now maximises tickets by the early 30s. The 2027 ballot closes 11 Oct 2026 (23:59 CEST).

### Annual re-plan and decision rules

**Plan:** Every December compare tests with the trajectories; more than 2 VDOT below 'likely' extends the phase; on the 'reach' path pulls targets forward.

**Evidence: ASSUMPTION** · Sources: [16]

Training response varies enormously between people (VO2max responses ranged from almost nothing to >1 L/min in HERITAGE) [16], so any decade plan must be re-anchored to measured results. The 2-VDOT threshold is my judgement: it is roughly one year of expected progress in the middle phases.

## B. Projection models (what the targets are built on)

### VDOT trajectory

**Plan:** Likely: 36 (now) -> 41 (2027) -> 43 (2028) -> 48 (2030) -> 51 (2032) -> 53 (2034). Reach: 36 -> 43 -> 46 -> 52 -> 55 -> 57.

**Evidence: ASSUMPTION** · Sources: [15], [58], [61]

There is no peer-reviewed data on yearly VDOT gains for adults who start running in their 20s; the research brief looked and flagged this gap. The path is built from: your measured novice gain (10K 1:07 in March to ~54-55 now, about +8 VDOT in 7 months) [61]; evidence that gains slow sharply toward the 650-900 h/yr plateau elite athletes reach after 10-15 years [15]; and smaller gains in the strength year when running drops to maintenance. All times are converted with the Daniels VDOT formula [58]. It will be checked against a mile and a 5K every year.

### Mile speed bias

**Plan:** Mile targets add +2.5 VDOT to the trajectory.

**Evidence: ASSUMPTION** · Sources: [23], [58], [61]

Your estimated mile (~7:20) is VDOT 38.7 against ~36 for 10K-to-half fitness [61][58], consistent with your first sprint session (842 W, ~8.3 m/s) and a large anaerobic speed reserve, which characterises speed-type middle-distance runners [23]. Whether the bias persists as aerobic fitness grows is unknown; the annual mile test checks it.

### Marathon padding

**Plan:** Marathon targets are 3-5% slower than the formula predicts from the half.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [17]

In a large sample of recreational runners, Riegel-type predictions were well calibrated from the mile to the half but too optimistic for the marathon [17]. A real marathon result will replace the estimate.

### Strength ladders

**Plan:** Bench 91 (e1RM now) -> 95-100 (Feb 2027) -> 105-110 (2028) -> 110-115 (2030) -> 115-124 (2034). Squat and deadlift are provisional until 2028 baselines.

**Evidence: ASSUMPTION** · Sources: [14], [51], [61]

Competitive powerlifters gain ~7.5-12.5% in year one and ~20% over ten years [14]; your bench has sat at 84-93 kg for six months in a deficit [61]. The ladder assumes faster gains in surplus/strength blocks and near-maintenance elsewhere. Advanced (80th percentile) at 75-80 kg is bench 117-124, squat 159-168, deadlift 186-196 [51]. There are no squat or deadlift baselines yet; the first strength block tests them.

### Sprint ladder

**Plan:** 100 m: timed baseline in Apr 2027, -2-3% by 2028, 12.6-12.9 (likely) / 12.3 (reach) by 2030; sub-12.0 is a dream goal.

**Evidence: ASSUMPTION** · Sources: [1], [18]

Specific sprint training improves times by about 3.2% per intervention, non-specific strength/plyometric training by about 2.6% [18]. Sub-12.0 from an estimated 13.5 s is ~11% faster and needs a top speed of roughly 9.3-9.6 m/s, while endurance training blunts explosive qualities [1]. The 13.5 s starting point is a GPS estimate, so no target is firm until a timed 30 m fly and 100 m exist.

### Separate sessions: cardio in the morning, lifting later or on non-run days

**Plan:** Never pair hard intervals and heavy legs in one session; keep >=3-6 h between sessions on the same day.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [1], [2], [53], [54], [55], [56], [59]

In trained lifters, lower-body strength gains were blunted when both modes were in the same session (ES -0.66) but not when sessions were more than 2 h apart (ES -0.10) [2]; explosive-strength interference was concentrated in same-session designs and disappeared at >=3 h apart [1]. If both are unavoidable in one session, lifting first protects strength (+6.9%) [59]. Practitioners differ here: OMNIA's example weeks pair lower-body lifting with intervals on the same day [53] and Nick Bare's programme runs daily doubles [54]; those only fit the evidence with hours between sessions, which is what Viada and Stronger by Science recommend (6-8 h) [55][56].

### Heavy, low-rep lower-body lifting during run blocks

**Plan:** Squat/hinge at >=80% 1RM, 3-5 reps, 4-8 hard sets/week.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [5], [6], [7]

Heavy loads improve running economy across 8.6-17.9 km/h, while 40-79% loads and isometrics did not [5], and high-load training improves time-trial performance [6]. Strength work improves economy and time trials without changing VO2max or body composition [7]. Keeping volume low limits the fatigue that interferes with running.

### Weekday swim on Monday or Wednesday morning, not Friday

**Plan:** Protect the Friday upper/bench session from lat and shoulder fatigue.

**Evidence: PRACTITIONER** · Sources: [56], [57]

No trial tests swim placement relative to bench sessions. The rationale is fatigue management: swimming loads the same lats and shoulders as the upper session, and practitioners recommend separating overlapping sessions as far as possible [56][57]. Low stakes; adjust if Friday sessions feel fine.

### Weekly fresh-day strides or flys

**Plan:** 6-8 x 60-80 m at full recovery once a week, never after lifting.

**Evidence: PRACTITIONER** · Sources: [1], [8], [20]

There is no direct trial of minimum sprint doses for maintaining speed in endurance athletes. The idea extends minimal-dose evidence from strength [8] and the observation that endurance work blunts explosive qualities [1]. Elite sprint programming organises speed work on fresh days with full recovery [20].

### HM block stays with Garmin Coach; targets from current fitness

**Plan:** Bangalore HM Dec 20: 2:02-2:06 likely, sub-2:00 reach.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [17], [58], [61]

The targets come from your measured tempo (4x5 min at 5:35-5:40 at threshold HR) and estimated VDOT ~36 [61], converted with the Daniels formula [58]. Formula predictions are well calibrated up to the half marathon [17]. Run programming stays with Garmin Coach, your considered choice, through the race.

### No calorie deficit while average sleep is under ~7 h

**Plan:** Cut only once the weekly sleep average reaches 7 h; target >=7.5 h.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [33], [34], [35], [61]

In a controlled deficit, 5.5 h vs 8.5 h of sleep meant 55% less fat lost and 60% more fat-free mass lost [33]; one night of sleep loss reduced muscle protein synthesis by 18% [34]. Sleep extension to ~8.5 h improved sprint times and skill in athletes [35]. Your average has been ~6.1-6.3 h [61], so dieting now would cost muscle.

### Bench block: higher upper-body volume, then peak

**Plan:** Dec 21 - Feb 14: upper body 12-14 sets/muscle/week for 5 weeks, 2 weeks of heavy singles/doubles, test week Feb 8-14.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [25], [26], [61]

Hypertrophy rises with weekly sets (~0.37% per added set; >=10 sets/week is the practical benchmark) [25], and both size and strength increase with volume, with strength showing stronger diminishing returns and benefiting from frequency [26]. Your current 2 sets/exercise is below that for pressing muscles [61]. The heavy weeks before the test follow the specificity principle; their exact structure is practitioner convention.

### Lean bulk during the bench block

**Plan:** +0.25-0.4% bodyweight/week (to ~79-80 kg).

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [27], [30]

A 10-20% surplus gaining about 0.25-0.5% of bodyweight per week is the recommended rate for novice-intermediate lifters to limit fat gain [27]. Protein near 1.6 g/kg is the point beyond which extra protein adds little muscle [30].

### Bench target 95-97.5 likely, 100 reach

**Plan:** Feb 2027 1RM test.

**Evidence: ASSUMPTION** · Sources: [14], [61]

From an e1RM of ~91 [61], +4-7% in eight weeks of surplus and higher volume is consistent with typical intermediate gains but faster than powerlifters' yearly average [14]. 100 kg needs about +10%, plausible only if the surplus and sleep both go well.

### Cut at 0.5-0.7%/week after the bench test

**Plan:** Feb 15 - Apr 25 to 76-77 kg; protein 150-180 g/day.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [28], [29]

Elite athletes losing 0.7%/week gained lean mass (+2.1%) and kept strength and sprint gains, while 1.4%/week lost lean mass [28]. In lean resistance-trained athletes, 2.3-3.1 g/kg fat-free mass protein is recommended during a deficit [29] (about 150-200 g for your ~65 kg lean mass).

### Start cycling in February; get a road bike and trainer

**Plan:** Bike 2-3x/week Z2 from Feb 2027, long ride building to 3 h by July.

**Evidence: PRACTITIONER** · Sources: [44]

Goa is a 1.9 km swim, 90 km bike and 21.1 km run [44]; the bike is the longest leg and you have no cycling history. Training specificity means the 90 km cannot be prepared on a gym bike alone (no bricks, handling or outdoor pacing). The exact build is practitioner convention, not tested.

### 80/20 intensity distribution in the triathlon base

**Plan:** About 80% of sessions easy, 20% threshold or harder.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [21], [22]

World-class middle-distance runners do about 80% of sessions at low intensity in a pyramidal pattern [21]. A meta-analysis found polarised training better for VO2peak only in short interventions in highly trained athletes, with time trials similar across distributions [22], so the low-intensity majority is supported while strict polarised vs pyramidal is not settled.

### 3 weeks loading, 1 week lighter

**Plan:** Run and triathlon mesocycles use 3:1 loading; lifting deloads every 4-6 weeks.

**Evidence: PRACTITIONER** · Sources: [42]

A Delphi consensus of strength coaches describes deloads of about 7 days every 4-6 weeks, cutting volume first [42]; it is expert consensus, not trial evidence. 3:1 endurance loading is common coaching practice with no direct trial support. Garmin Coach applies its own adaptive easy weeks.

### Energy availability on big training days

**Plan:** Eat to the day's training load; avoid flat 2,500 kcal on long-run and brick days.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [31], [32], [61]

Energy availability of ~45 kcal/kg fat-free mass/day approximates balance and <30 is associated with impairments [31]; in males the threshold may be lower but REDs risk is real [32]. At ~65 kg lean mass, a heavy training day at 2,500 kcal approaches that line [61]. Low energy availability and iron deficiency overlap in the REDs model [32], which matters given your ferritin of 27.

### Optional Olympic-distance tune-up in the 70.3 base

**Plan:** One Olympic-distance race (1.5 / 40 / 10 km) in summer 2027, target 2:40-2:55.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [46]

A shorter race rehearses transitions, open-water swimming and pacing before Goa. In age-group Olympic-distance data the men's average is about 2:53 and the top 20% is around 2:35 [46]; 2:40-2:55 places a first-year triathlete near the average, consistent with the Goa projection. Whether to race it is your call; the evidence for tune-up races specifically is practitioner experience.

### Two-week taper before key races

**Plan:** Cut volume 41-60% over 2 weeks, keep intensity.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [39]

A meta-analysis found the best taper lasts ~2 weeks with volume reduced exponentially by 41-60% and no reduction in intensity (volume-reduction effect size 0.72) [39].

### Race fuelling 60-90 g carbohydrate per hour

**Plan:** Practise in long rides and bricks from August; glucose + fructose mixes.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [40]

Glucose absorption saturates around 60 g/h; glucose-fructose mixtures raise exogenous carbohydrate oxidation to ~90 g/h, recommended for events over 2-2.5 h, with less fatigue and better performance [40]. A 70.3 lasts ~6 h for you.

### Heat acclimation before Goa

**Plan:** 10-14 days of training in heat before race week (Bengaluru afternoons work).

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [41]

Most heat adaptations (heart rate, core temperature, sweat rate) occur in the first week and are complete by 10-14 days; sessions should be >=30 min on consecutive days [41].

### Goa 70.3 target 5:50-6:20 likely, sub-5:35 reach

**Plan:** First 70.3, November 2027.

**Evidence: ASSUMPTION** · Sources: [44], [61]

Built from: swim ~45-48 min (1.9 km at your CSS-based endurance pace of ~2:26/100 m [61]); bike ~3:00-3:20 for a first-year cyclist (my assumption, no data); run ~1:55-2:05 off the bike in heat; transitions ~8 min. The men 25-29 median is 5:39:26 and the whole-field top 25% is under 5:16:29 [44], so the reach target is around the median, appropriate for a first attempt.

### DEXA scan before the February cut

**Plan:** One DEXA scan for a lean-mass/fat baseline.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [43]

Consumer bioelectrical impedance scales systematically underestimate fat mass and overestimate fat-free mass compared with DXA [43], so body-composition targets set from your BIA scale (~16% fat) could be off by several percent.

### Four lifting days (P/A/P/A) with 12-16 sets per muscle in the hypertrophy block

**Plan:** Double frequency and raise volume while running drops to maintenance.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [25], [26], [57], [61]

Muscle growth rises with weekly set volume [25][26] and strength benefits from training frequency [26]. Endurance training hurts gaining muscle more than keeping it [57], so the year with the lowest running volume is the one to push hypertrophy. Your own split document already names P/A/P/A as the better programme for off-season [61].

### Strength block with heavy waves and planned deloads

**Plan:** 3-6 reps, top sets at RPE 8-9, 4-week waves, deload every 4-6 weeks.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [26], [42]

Strength responds to heavier loads with steeper diminishing returns on volume than hypertrophy [26]. Deload timing follows the strength-coach consensus [42]. The specific wave structure is practitioner convention.

### Acceleration and resisted sprints once a week

**Plan:** Short accelerations (10-30 m), resisted sprints, full recovery.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [18], [19], [20]

Resisted sprints have a large effect on short sprint times (ES -1.39) [18] and improve 10 m acceleration but not maximum velocity [19]. Elite sprint training builds acceleration first, then maximum velocity [20]. Pairing it with the strength year uses the strength gains as they arrive.

### Running kept at 30-40 km/week

**Plan:** Three runs a week, mostly easy, plus strides.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [3], [8]

Interference with strength grows with endurance frequency and duration [3], so cutting running volume during the strength year reduces it. The exact volume needed to hold aerobic fitness is not well established; this is a conservative judgement backed by the general minimal-dose principle [8].

### Bulk to ~80 kg, then cut to 77

**Plan:** +0.25%/week for 16 weeks, then 0.5-0.7%/week.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [27], [28]

Same evidence as Phase 1: recommended gain rates [27] and slow cut rates that preserve lean mass and strength [28].

### Home weight 75-77 kg at 10-12% fat

**Plan:** Target fat-free mass index ~19.7-20.7 at 182.9 cm.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [36], [37], [38]

The target requires roughly +0-3 kg lean mass and -6 to -9 kg fat. The resulting FFMI sits below the drug-free mean of 21.8 [38], so it is achievable naturally. Lower body fat predicts faster marathon and Ironman times [36][37].

### Rates of change

**Plan:** Cut 0.5-0.7%/week; gain 0.25-0.5%/week.

**Evidence: MODERATE** · Sources: [27], [28]

Slow cuts preserve lean mass and performance [28]; recommended lean-gain rates limit fat gain [27].

### Protein

**Plan:** 150-180 g/day throughout; top of the range in deficits.

**Evidence: STRONG** · Sources: [29], [30], [31]

1.2-2.0 g/kg/day for athletes, higher in deficits [31]; hypertrophy benefit plateaus near 1.6 g/kg [30]; 2.3-3.1 g/kg fat-free mass in lean athletes during deficits [29].
