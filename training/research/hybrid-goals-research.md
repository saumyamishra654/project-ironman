# Hybrid Goals: What the Research Says

*Research brief for Saumya, October 2026. Covers concurrent-training structure, how fast he can expect to improve, sprint development, sequencing goals over a decade, body composition, and practical prescriptions. Every number comes with a link. Lines marked "my arithmetic" are calculations, not cited facts.*

---

## Executive summary

The overall plan holds up: endurance-first, with strength kept going alongside. The research mostly supports it. The real risks are three things. The running targets are a much bigger jump than the plan admits. Sub-12 100 m and heavy lower-body strength numbers clash with high running volume. And sleep is currently the weakest link.

1. **Interference is small if sessions are separated.** Across 43 studies, adding aerobic work did not reduce gains in maximal strength (SMD −0.06) or muscle size (SMD −0.01). It did reduce explosive strength (SMD −0.28), mostly when both were done in the same session ([Schumann 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/)). In *trained* lifters, lower-body 1RM gains do suffer, but only when both are done in the same session (ES −0.66). With sessions more than 2 h apart the effect is ES −0.10, not significant ([Petré 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053170/)). His current setup already avoids this: all cardio in the morning and lifting on separate days.
2. **Heavy strength work helps the running goals directly.** Heavy lifting (>80% 1RM) improves running economy across 8.64–17.85 km/h ([Llanos-Lagos 2024a](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01978-y)) and improves time-trial performance more than plyometrics do ([Llanos-Lagos 2024b](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02018-z)). Keep the squat and deadlift heavy rather than high-rep.
3. **The 95th-percentile running goals need +17 to +22 VDOT from today's ~36** (my arithmetic, Daniels formula). That is a multi-year project, not a two-year one. Elite runners usually train 8–10+ years before reaching their level ([long-term development scoping review 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10356634/)). His novice gains (10K 1:07 in March → ~54–55 now) will slow down sharply.
4. **Sub-12.0 100 m is the least realistic goal.** Dedicated sprint training produces about 3% improvement per intervention ([Rumpf 2016](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2016/06000/effect_of_different_sprint_training_methods_on.35.aspx)). Going from 13.5 to under 12.0 is about an 11% drop in time, and endurance training specifically blunts explosive qualities. This goal needs its own dedicated block and a properly timed baseline. A GPS estimate isn't one.
5. **Lower-body strength targets clash most with the running targets.** Males show blunted lower-body strength gains under concurrent training (SMD −0.43) ([Huiberts 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933151/)). Squatting about 160–168 kg and deadlifting about 186–196 kg at 75–77 kg bodyweight while running a sub-1:25 HM is rare. Treat these as the off-season strength-block targets, not as targets to chase alongside run peaks.
6. **Fix sleep before cutting.** In a calorie deficit, 5.5 h vs 8.5 h of sleep meant 55% less fat lost and 60% more fat-free mass lost ([Nedeltcheva 2010](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/abs/10.7326/0003-4819-153-7-201010050-00006)). One night of total sleep loss cuts muscle protein synthesis by 18% ([Lamon 2021](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.14814/phy2.14660)). His ~6.1–6.3 h is the main threat to a lean cut.
7. **The body-composition target is reasonable.** At 75–77 kg and 10–12% fat he would have an FFMI of about 19.7–20.7 (my arithmetic). That is below the drug-free mean of 21.8 ([Kouri 1995, via summary](https://ffmicheck.com/blog/natural-genetic-muscle-limit-science/)). Cut slowly, at about 0.7% of bodyweight per week ([Garthe 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51113664_Effect_of_Two_Different_Weight-Loss_Rates_on_Body_Composition_and_Strength_and_Power-Related_Performance_in_Elite_Athletes)).
8. **Norseman: the 2027 ballot closes Oct 11, 2026.** Each consecutive unsuccessful year adds a ticket: 1, then 2, then 3 ([nxtri.com](https://www.nxtri.com/registration-slots)). Entering every year from now builds odds for a 30–35 attempt, but the counter resets if he skips a year.

## 1. Concurrent / hybrid training structure

### Evidence

| Study | What it found |
|---|---|
| [Wilson 2012, JSCR](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/Fulltext/2012/08000/Concurrent_Training___A_Meta_Analysis_Examining.35.aspx) (21 studies) | Strength training alone vs concurrent: hypertrophy ES 1.22 vs 0.80, strength 1.71 vs 1.28. Running interfered; cycling did not. Interference rose with endurance **frequency** (r −0.26 to −0.35) and **duration** (r −0.29 to −0.75) ([summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51719597_Concurrent_Training_A_Meta-Analysis_Examining_Interference_of_Aerobic_and_Resistance_Exercises)). |
| [Petré 2021, Sports Med](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053170/) | Lower-body 1RM: trained ES −0.35; moderately trained −0.20 (ns); untrained 0.03. Trained, same session: −0.66. Trained, sessions >2 h apart: −0.10 (ns). |
| [Schumann 2022, Sports Med](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/) (43 studies) | Max strength SMD −0.06 (ns); hypertrophy −0.01 (ns); explosive strength −0.28 (p = 0.007), and −0.31 in the same session. No interference when sessions were ≥3 h apart. Running vs cycling was not a significant moderator. |
| [Huiberts 2023, Sports Med](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933151/) | Lower-body strength blunted in **males** (SMD −0.43) but not in females. VO₂max gains blunted only in *untrained* endurance athletes (−0.35). |
| [Eddens 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-017-0784-1) / [Murlasits 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29658408/) | Within one session, lifting first gave +6.91% lower-body dynamic strength (≈3.96 kg). No other outcome was affected. |
| [Held et al. 2026 umbrella review, Sports Med](https://fis.dshs-koeln.de/en/publications/maximizing-adaptations-in-concurrent-training-an-umbrella-review-/) (17 meta-analyses, 144 studies) | Concurrent vs strength-only: strength, power and hypertrophy comparable. Concurrent vs endurance-only: aerobic capacity comparable. Session order: no significant effect, with a trend favouring lifting first for strength. Data on highly trained athletes are "scarce". I read only the abstract and a summary; the full text was paywalled. |

**Strength training for runners:**
- [Blagrove 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-017-0835-7) (24 studies, 469 trained runners): strength work improved running economy, time trials and max sprint speed, without changing VO₂max or body composition. The likely mechanisms are neural and tendon stiffness.
- [Llanos-Lagos 2024a](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01978-y) (running economy): heavy loads (>80% 1RM) help at 8.64–17.85 km/h, with larger effects at higher speeds and higher VO₂max. Plyometrics help mainly below 12 km/h. Submaximal (40–79%) and isometric training **did not** improve economy ([summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376260720_Effect_of_Strength_Training_Programs_in_Middle-_and_Long-Distance_Runners'_Economy_at_Different_Running_Speeds_A_Systematic_Review_with_Meta-analysis)).
- [Llanos-Lagos 2024b](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02018-z) found high-load training improves time-trial performance.

**Intensity distribution:**
- [Oliveira 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11329428/) (17 studies, n = 437): polarised training beat other distributions for VO₂peak, but only in interventions under 12 weeks and only in highly trained athletes. Time-trial results were similar across distributions.
- In world-class 1500 m runners, about 80% of sessions are low intensity in a *pyramidal* pattern ([Haugen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8363530/)).
- "80/20 easy" is well supported. Strict polarised vs pyramidal is not settled.

### What practitioners say

| Coach | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **OMNIA (Fergus Crawley)** | 3–5 runs (1–3 easy, 1 long, 1–2 interval/tempo) plus 2–3 gym sessions. Example week pairs lower-body lifting with intervals on the same day. Hypertrophy early in the block, heavier and lower-volume lifting closer to the race ([marathon + strength](https://www.omnia-performance.com/blog/how-to-build-strength-and-train-for-a-marathon/)). Their interference article only says "lifting before cardio" ([source](https://www.omnia-performance.com/blog/the-interference-effect/)). |
| **Nick Bare / BPN** | About 5 lifts + 4 runs per week. Most days are doubles: run in the morning, lift later. One speed run, one long run, the rest easy ([program summary](https://liftvault.com/programs/running/nick-bare-16-week-hybrid-training-program-spreadsheet/)). Compound lift first. "Training sessions must be intense" ([BPN](https://www.bareperformancenutrition.com/blogs/content/the-6-pillars-of-the-hybrid-build)). |
| **Alex Viada (CHP)** | Order: "Highest skill first, most explosive next, lowest power, highest energy cost last". Separate same-day sessions by 6–8 h ([Rox Lyfe interview](https://roxlyfe.com/alex-viada/)). Hybrid goals "may take 50 percent longer"; "prioritize one or the other" first ([BarBend](https://barbend.com/alex-viada-hybrid-training-interview/)). |
| **Stronger by Science** | Interference "isn't that big of a deal". Be cautious if recovery is compromised (poor sleep, big deficit), endurance volume is already maxed, or power/speed is the priority ([SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-interference-effect/)). Avoid long cardio right after lifting; recovery runs 6–8 h after or the next morning ([SBS practical](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/practical-considerations-for-combining-cardiovascular-training-and-lifting/)). |
| **Renaissance Periodization** | Endurance training hurts *gaining* muscle far more than *keeping* it. Protein 0.7–1.0 g/lb. Keep lifting and endurance "as far separated" as possible. Prefer cycling over running. "Maximize sleep" ([RP](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/how-endurance-training-affects-muscle-retention-and-growth)). |

### Conflicts and weak evidence
- **Same-day pairing.** OMNIA's same-day pairing (lower body + intervals) and Bare's daily doubles go against the best-supported finding: same-session interference in trained lifters (Petré, Schumann). They are defensible only with hours between sessions, which matches Viada's 6–8 h.
- **"Cardio doesn't hurt gains"** (BPN) holds for size and max strength, but not for explosive strength. It also doesn't hold for trained males' lower-body strength (Huiberts, Petré).
- **RP's "cycling over running"** matches Wilson 2012 but not Schumann 2022, where modality was not a moderator. The evidence is mixed.
- **Thin evidence where it matters most.** Most trials are 8–12 weeks long and use untrained or recreational subjects at volumes far below 40–70 km/week. Almost no trial tests 95th-percentile endurance plus advanced strength.

### What this means for Saumya
- His layout already follows the strongest finding: all cardio in the morning, lifting M/W/F, runs T/Th/Sa/Su. Keep hard run sessions **off** lift days.
- The weekday swim pulls with the lats and shoulders. Put it on **Monday or Wednesday morning**, not Friday, so it doesn't drain the Friday Upper/bench session.
- Make lower-body lifting **heavy and low-rep** (e.g., 3–5 reps at RPE 8, 2–3 sets). That gives the running-economy benefit at the lowest fatigue cost.

## 2. Time course: how fast can he improve?

### Evidence

**Running:**
- VO₂max response to 20 weeks of training averaged about 400 mL/min. Some people gained almost nothing and others more than 1.0 L/min, with 2.5× more variance between families than within them ([HERITAGE, Bouchard 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10484570/)). Trainability is partly genetic.
- Elite endurance athletes needed "10–15 years" of dedicated training. Annual volume rose non-linearly to a 650–900 h plateau. Threshold and economy kept improving; VO₂max did so inconsistently ([long-term development scoping review 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10356634/)).
- Younger runners with fewer training years improved with heavy training *without* VO₂max change. Older, long-trained runners did not improve ([PubMed 16446673](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16446673/)). Training age matters more than calendar age.
- World-class track athletes peak at 25–27 (marathoners around 28–29). In the 5 years before peak they improve only a few percent; throwers, the biggest improvers, gained 7.0% ([Haugen 2018, IJSPP](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2017-0682)). Those are near-ceiling athletes, not novices.
- **Gap:** I found no peer-reviewed long-term study of VDOT gain per year in adult-onset recreational runners. Websites quoting "3–5 VDOT in the first 6–12 months" are not research.

**Strength:**
- Competitive powerlifters gained about 7.5–12.5% in year one and about 20% after 10 years ([Latella 2020, 15-year analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342820765_Long-Term_Strength_Adaptation_A_15-Year_Analysis_of_Powerlifting_Athletes)).
- Across 6,524 classic lifters, the cohort peaked at about age 27 and individuals peaked after 4–6 years of competing ([Renner 2026, BMC SSMR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13418126/)).

### Where his goals sit (my arithmetic, Daniels VDOT formula)

| Target | VDOT needed | vs his ~36 now |
|---|---|---|
| Current: 10K ~54:30 / HM ~2:03 / mile 7:20 | 36.2 / 35.4 / 38.7 | — |
| 5K sub-19 · 10K sub-39 · HM sub-1:25 · M sub-3:00 | 52.9 · 53.5 · 54.5 · 53.5 | **+17–18** |
| 5K 17:40 · 10K 36:38 · HM 1:21 · M 2:49 · mile 5:08 | 57.6–57.7 | **+21–22** |
| Marathon 3:10 | 50.2 | +14 |

Two observations:
- **His mile is his strongest event** (VDOT 38.7 vs 36 for the longer races), consistent with a speed-biased profile.
- **Garmin's VO₂max of 46 is about 10 points above his race-equivalent VDOT.** Garmin's number isn't VDOT, but a gap that large suggests economy, threshold fraction and durability are the limiters, not aerobic ceiling. Those respond to volume, years and heavy strength. This is an interpretation, not a citation.

### Practitioner view
Viada: hybrid goals "may take 50 percent longer" ([BarBend](https://barbend.com/alex-viada-hybrid-training-interview/)). No coach source I found gives evidence-based year-by-year rates.

### Conflicts and weak evidence
- Riegel-style predictions are "well-calibrated" from the mile to the half but "too optimistic" for the marathon ([Vickers 2016](https://bmcsportsscimedrehabil.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13102-016-0052-y)). The marathon targets should be judged by an actual marathon, not by HM equivalence.

### What this means for Saumya
- **Expected pace:** about 19% faster 10K in 7 months (1:07 → ~54–55). The next 2–3 years will plausibly add several VDOT points per year, then 1–2 per year after that (estimate, not a citation).
- **Race-population 95th-percentile targets (VDOT ~53–55):** a realistic target window is **age 27–31**, only with years of 50–80 km/week.
- **Running Level 95th-percentile targets (VDOT ~58):** not guaranteed for anyone. Treat them as stretch goals.
- **Bench 91 → 117–124 kg** is +29–36%. That is more than powerlifters' typical first-year gains and close to their 10-year gains. Plan on years, not one block.

## 3. Speed and sprint development

### Evidence
- **Sprint training effects** ([Rumpf 2016](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2016/06000/effect_of_different_sprint_training_methods_on.35.aspx), 48 studies): specific sprint training ES −1.00, about 3.23% faster. Non-specific training (strength, plyometrics) ES −0.32, about 2.63%. Resisted sprints had a large effect (ES −1.39) ([summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/303522049_BRIEF_REVIEW_EFFECT_OF_DIFFERENT_SPRINT_TRAINING_METHODS_ON_SPRINT_PERFORMANCE_OVER_VARIOUS_DISTANCES_A_BRIEF_REVIEW)).
- **Resisted sprints** improve 10 m acceleration but **not** maximum velocity. Uphill–downhill combined training looked better for max velocity ([2024 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11469994/)).
- **Elite sprint training** is organised around acceleration, then max velocity, then speed endurance ([Haugen 2019](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-019-0221-0)).
- **Endurance blunts explosive qualities,** especially in the same session (SMD −0.28; same session −0.31) ([Schumann 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/)).
- **Middle distance:** the 1500 m is 75–85% aerobic. World-class milers run 120–170 km/week in preparation, and the best middle-distance runners "may approach 10 m·s⁻¹" top speed ([Haugen 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8363530/)).
- **Speed reserve:** anaerobic speed reserve (max sprint speed minus max aerobic speed) separates 800 m profiles. Speed-type athletes need more lactate-tolerance work, endurance types more aerobic volume ([Sandford](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328186651_Anaerobic_Speed_Reserve_A_Key_Component_of_Elite_Male_800m_Running)).

### What practitioners say
Viada prefers "small doses of plyometrics before harder runs or speed sessions" ([Rox Lyfe](https://roxlyfe.com/alex-viada/)). SBS advises extra caution when speed or power is the priority ([SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-interference-effect/)).

### Conflicts and weak evidence
- Almost all sprint trials are short (6–12 weeks) and use team-sport athletes.
- I found no good data on multi-year max-velocity gains in adults who start sprinting in their 20s.
- Adult trainability of top speed appears modest. That is an inference from the small per-block effects, not a direct finding.

### What this means for Saumya
- **The 100 m baseline is soft.** 8.3 m/s is a GPS peak, and a 12.0 s 100 m already *averages* 8.33 m/s. Sub-12 likely needs a top speed around 9.3–9.6 m/s, roughly +12–16% (my arithmetic).
- **Get a real baseline:** a timed 30 m fly and 100 m (phone video or timing gates) before setting the target.
- **Sub-60 400 m and sub-5:08 mile are realistic** in the 24–27 speed window. A speed-biased athlete gets there faster from aerobic volume than from more sprinting. The mile needs about VDOT 58 (see table).
- **Within run weeks, keep a minimum dose:** 6–8 × 60–80 m sprints or strides at full recovery, once a week, on a fresh day (not after lifting). That keeps top speed and fits the anterior-chain lift.

## 4. Sequencing over a decade

### Age of peak

| Event | Peak age | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint / power | about 20–27; around 25 | [Allen & Hopkins 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-015-0354-3) |
| Track overall | 25–27; marathon 28–29 | [Haugen 2018](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2017-0682) |
| Ironman | 32.2 ± 1.5 (men) | [Knechtle, Extreme Physiol Med](https://extremephysiolmed.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/2046-7648-2-27) |
| Ironman | 35.1 (men, 2003–13) | [Knechtle, SpringerPlus](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/2193-1801-3-538) |
| Classic powerlifting | about 27 (cohort peak) | [Renner 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13418126/) |

Caveat: all of these are *calendar* ages of lifelong athletes. Someone who starts at 22 peaks later in calendar years for endurance events, because training age drives endurance development (see Q2).

### Focused blocks vs doing everything at once
- **Block periodisation** of endurance: small advantage for VO₂max (ES 0.40) and Wmax (SMD 0.28). The studies were low quality (mean PEDro 3.7/10) and covered weeks, not years ([Mølmen 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31802956)).
- **Maintaining strength:** 1 session/week, 1 set per exercise at high effort, keeps 1RM for 8–32 weeks in 20–35-year-olds ([Spiering 2021](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2021/05000/maintaining_physical_performance__the_minimal_dose.35.aspx)). Well-trained cyclists kept strength and thigh CSA gains over 13 weeks of competition with once-weekly heavy lifting ([Rønnestad 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/46010265_In-season_strength_maintenance_training_increases_well-trained_cyclists'_performance)).
- **Practitioner view:** Viada says "prioritize one or the other" ([BarBend](https://barbend.com/alex-viada-hybrid-training-interview/)). RP says gain muscle first, then add endurance ([RP](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/how-endurance-training-affects-muscle-retention-and-growth)).
- **Evidence gap:** no study tests decade-scale goal sequencing. The supported principle is "develop one quality, maintain the others cheaply."

### Norseman specifics
- **Course:** 3.8 km fjord swim; 180 km bike with "more than 3000 meters of climbing"; marathon finishing on Gaustatoppen. Field of 250–255 starters ([nxtri.com](https://www.nxtri.com/this-is-norsman)). Water has been as cold as 10 °C (2015) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norseman_triathlon)).
- **Black T-shirt:** the official page now says "The 160 first athletes to reach 37 k Stavsro" finish on the summit ([nxtri.com](https://www.nxtri.com/this-is-norsman)). Older sources give a 32.5 km / 14:30 cut-off ([summary](https://www.hardastrails.com/norseman-xtreme-triathlon/)). The rule appears to have changed, so check the current race manual.
- **Entry:**
  - 2027 race: Sat 7 Aug 2027. Ballot: Oct 1–11 2026 (closes 23:59 CEST), draw Oct 14, results Oct 21 (20:00 CEST, YouTube). **125 of 335 start slots go through the ballot.** Ballot fee EUR 0.50 + platform fee. Entry fee if drawn: EUR 550, payable by Nov 14. Must be 18+. (Verified against nxtri.com/registration-slots, Oct 5 2026.)
  - Tickets: 1 in the first year, 2 after one consecutive miss, 3 after two. The counter resets if drawn or if you skip a year.
  - Other routes: 100 X-Points slots and 57 XTRI World Tour slots.
  - No prior long-distance finish is explicitly required ([nxtri.com](https://www.nxtri.com/registration-slots)).
- **Finisher backgrounds:** I could not find systematic data. A black shirt means being roughly in the top 60% of the field at 37 km. Hill climbing on the bike, cold-water tolerance and mountain hiking/running matter more than flat speed.

### What this means for Saumya
- The proposed sequence (base → speed window 24–27 → long-distance goals 27–31 → Norseman 30–35) **lines up well** with peak-age data and with training-age logic.
- **One tweak to the speed window:** keep a 2×/week heavy lifting maintenance dose through it. Better still, make **24–25 the main strength block**. Strength peaks around 27, and heavy strength *helps* the speed goals, so this beats saving strength for 27–31.
- **Enter the Norseman ballot every year from now on.** The ticket build-up rewards consecutive entries, and each costs about EUR 1.50. Before entering, decide whether to decline if drawn early: a decline might reset the counter. The page says it resets "when drawn".

## 5. Body composition

### Evidence
- **Marathon:** in recreational male marathoners, % body fat and training speed together predicted race time: *time (min) = 326.3 + 2.394 × %BF − 12.06 × training speed (km/h)*. The model explained 44% of variance; training speed alone explained 40% ([Barandun 2012](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.2147/OAJSM.S33284)).
- **Ironman:** in male Ironman triathletes, %BF was associated with race time (r² = 0.583). It was not associated in females ([Knechtle 2010](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02701367.2010.10599628)).
- **Rate of loss:** losing 0.7%/week vs 1.4%/week (both at about 1.6 g/kg protein) gave +2.1% lean mass vs −0.2%, plus better 1RM and sprint gains ([Garthe 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51113664_Effect_of_Two_Different_Weight-Loss_Rates_on_Body_Composition_and_Strength_and_Power-Related_Performance_in_Elite_Athletes)).
- **Protein in a deficit:** 2.3–3.1 g/kg fat-free mass for lean, resistance-trained athletes ([Helms 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257350851_A_Systematic_Review_of_Dietary_Protein_During_Caloric_Restriction_in_Resistance_Trained_Lean_Athletes_A_Case_for_Higher_Intakes)). Note that only 2 of the 6 studies compared protein doses directly.
- **Rate of gain:** about 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight/week with a 10–20% surplus for novice and intermediate lifters ([Iraki 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334044740_Nutrition_Recommendations_for_Bodybuilders_in_the_Off-Season_A_Narrative_Review)).
- **Natural muscle ceiling:** drug-free athletes had a mean FFMI of 21.8 ± 1.8 and none exceeded 25 ([Kouri 1995, secondary summary](https://ffmicheck.com/blog/natural-genetic-muscle-limit-science/); I did not open the primary paper).

### Conflicts and weak evidence
- The Barandun and Knechtle findings are cross-sectional. Leaner runners also train more and faster, so applying the 2.394 min/%BF slope to one person's cut (e.g., 17% → 11% "= 14 min") overstates it.
- BIA body-fat readings are noisy. Use the trend in the scale weight plus waist measurement.

### Sanity check on the target (my arithmetic)

| | Lean mass | FFMI (height 1.829 m) |
|---|---|---|
| Now: 79 kg at 16–19% fat | ~64–66 kg | ~19.1–19.8 |
| Target: 75–77 kg at 10–12% fat | ~66–69 kg | ~19.7–20.7 |

- The target means **+0–3 kg of lean mass and −6 to −9 kg of fat**. That is achievable for a natural lifter.
- It is a lean but not extreme build for a 2:49–3:00 marathoner at BMI about 22.5–23.
- **The lower end, 75 kg at 12%, is the better fit with the running goals. The upper end, 77 kg at 10%, fits the lifting goals better.**

### What this means for Saumya
- Cut at **0.5–0.7% of bodyweight per week** (about 0.4–0.55 kg/week) with protein around **150–180 g/day** (2.3–2.7 g/kg of his ~65 kg FFM). His current ~169 g/day is already in this range.
- Don't run the cut during the HM taper or in the 70.3 peak weeks.
- Don't push it while average sleep is under about 7 h (see §6).

## 6. Practical prescriptions

### Lifting volume
- **Hypertrophy and volume:**
  - Each extra weekly set was worth about 0.37% more muscle growth; ≥10 sets/muscle/week is the usual practical benchmark ([Schoenfeld 2017](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Dose-response-relationship-between-weekly-training-Schoenfeld-Ogborn/0d34206f962394983054451cddd8a3b91818f732)).
  - [Pelland 2024/25](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460) (67 studies): both size and strength rise with volume, but "the diminishing returns for strength being considerably more pronounced". Frequency mattered for strength, not size. Indirect sets count as half.
- **For him** (my recommendation, consistent with the above and with Viada's "least amount of volume needed"):
  - **Upper body:** 10–14 fractional sets/week for chest, shoulders and triceps. Upper body doesn't compete with running, and bench is the near-term goal. Back can stay lower, because 2–3 swims a week already load the lats.
  - **Lower body:** 4–8 hard sets/week, mostly heavy (≥80% 1RM), during run-build phases.
  - **Off-season strength blocks:** raise lower body to 10+.
  - Today's 2 sets per exercise across a 3-day split probably lands at about 6–10 sets for most muscles. His bench e1RM has sat at 84–93 kg for 6 months (training/CLAUDE.md). The cheapest fix is a third set on the pressing lifts, and keeping both weekly barbell-bench sessions heavy.

### Same-day order
Lift first if both happen in one session. Better still, keep them ≥3–6 h apart or on different days ([Eddens 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-017-0784-1); [Petré 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053170/); [Schumann 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8891239/)). Easy morning runs before evening lifts are fine, per SBS and Viada.

### Sleep
- [Mah 2011](https://aasm.org/extended-sleep-improves-the-athletic-performance-of-collegiate-basketball-players/): basketball players went from about 6.7 h to about 8.5 h of sleep (≥10 h in bed). Sprint time dropped from 16.2 s to 15.5 s, and free throws and 3-pointers each improved by 9%.
- In adolescent athletes, under 8 h of sleep was linked to 1.7× injury risk ([Milewski 2014](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263971781_Chronic_Lack_of_Sleep_is_Associated_With_Increased_Sports_Injuries_in_Adolescent_Athletes)).
- In a deficit, short sleep shifts weight loss from fat to lean mass ([Nedeltcheva 2010](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/abs/10.7326/0003-4819-153-7-201010050-00006)), and acute sleep loss cuts muscle protein synthesis by 18%, cortisol +21%, testosterone −24% ([Lamon 2021](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.14814/phy2.14660)).
- **For him:** at 6.1–6.3 h, sleep is the highest-return fix. His existing 22:30 lights-out / 06:30 wake protocol gives an 8 h window. The weekly target should be **≥7.5 h average asleep**.

### Nutrition

| Item | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Protein | 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day generally, higher during deficits or hard training ([ACSM/AND/DC 2016](https://sky.sausport.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/American-College-of-Sports-Medicine_Joint-Position_Nutrition_and_Athletic_Performance_2016.pdf)). For muscle gain, the benefit plateaus at about 1.62 g/kg (CI up to about 2.2) ([Morton 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318368028_A_systematic_review_meta-analysis_and_meta-regression_of_the_effect_of_protein_supplementation_on_resistance_training-induced_gains_in_muscle_mass_and_strength_in_healthy_adults)). RP's 0.7–1.0 g/lb (1.5–2.2 g/kg) matches. |
| Carbohydrate | ACSM 2016: 3–5 g/kg for light training; 5–7 for moderate (~1 h/day); 6–10 for endurance at 1–3 h/day; 8–12 for 4–5 h/day. |
| Train-low | No overall performance benefit vs normal high-carb training in trained athletes ([Gejl & Nybo 2021](https://jissn.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12970-021-00435-3)). Not worth the cost for a hybrid athlete. |
| Energy availability (RED-S) | 45 kcal/kg FFM/day ≈ energy balance; below 30 is associated with impairments ([ACSM 2016](https://sky.sausport.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/American-College-of-Sports-Medicine_Joint-Position_Nutrition_and_Athletic_Performance_2016.pdf)). In males the threshold "appears to be lower (eg, ~9 to 25 kcal/kg FFM/day)" ([IOC 2023 REDs](https://stillmed.olympics.com/media/Documents/Athletes/Medical-Scientific/Consensus-Statements/REDs/BJSM-IOC-consensus-statement-on-Relative-Energy-Deficiency-in-Sport-REDs.pdf), via [summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11561064/)). |

**For him:**
- At about 65 kg FFM, energy availability of 30 kcal/kg works out to roughly **1,950 kcal/day left after training**.
- At 2,500 kcal/day, a heavy week burning 800–1,000+ kcal/day in training drops him close to that line (my arithmetic).
- **Low energy availability can also interact with his confirmed low ferritin.** Iron deficiency and LEA overlap in the REDs model. Eat more on long-run and brick days rather than holding a flat 2,500.

## Recommended adjustments to the plan

- **Keep the current concurrent layout.** Morning cardio and lifting on separate days is what the evidence supports. Don't adopt Bare/OMNIA-style lift-plus-intervals doubles on the same day.
- **Move the weekday swim to Monday or Wednesday morning,** so the Friday Upper/bench session is fresh.
- **Make lower-body lifting heavy and low-rep** (≥80% 1RM, 4–8 sets/week) during run blocks. Push upper body to 10–14 sets/week through the bench-100 block.
- **Add one weekly session of 6–8 full-recovery strides or flys** on a fresh day to keep top speed. **Get a timed 30 m fly and 100 m baseline** before keeping sub-12.0 as a goal.
- **Re-label the running targets:**
  - Race-population 95th percentile (VDOT ~53–55): realistic for **27–31**.
  - Running Level 95th percentile (VDOT ~58): stretch goal.
  - Use a mile and 5K test each year to track VDOT.
- **Re-label the lower-body strength targets** (squat about 160–168 kg, deadlift about 186–196 kg) as **off-season strength-block goals**. Don't chase them alongside run peaks. Consider making age 24–25 a strength-emphasis year, with 1–2 heavy sessions/week as maintenance during speed and run blocks.
- **Sleep ≥7.5 h average before starting any deficit.** Then cut at 0.5–0.7%/week, with protein around 150–180 g/day and carbs scaled to the day's training load.
- **Enter the Norseman ballot every October** (2027 ballot closes Oct 11 2026) to build tickets, and check the current black-shirt rule (37 km Stavsro, first 160) in the race manual.
